Merck Veterinary Manual · Chocolate Toxicosis in Animals — updated February 2026; reviewed November 3, 2026. Concentrations and canine dose references come from this page.

Emesis timing: the up-to-4-hour window is the clinician-selected reference for this site. Merck describes recent exposure (eg, within 2 hours); this broader window is not attributed to Merck. Suitability depends on the patient’s clinical condition. Activated-charcoal guidance retains Merck’s restriction to lethal exposures after weighing risks.

For a known cocoa percentage, this calculator uses 15.5 × cocoa % ÷ 100 mg/g. This is an estimate, not a product assay.

Weingart C, Hartmann A, Kohn B. Chocolate ingestion in dogs: 156 events (2015–2019). J Small Anim Pract. 2021;62:979–983. doi:10.1111/jsap.13329. The abstract reports 44 dogs with clinical signs and 112 without; 43 of the 44 affected dogs survived after treatment. This observational series supports the clinical context, not a guaranteed outcome or validation of this calculator’s thresholds or treatment choices. Abstract reviewed; full text was not verified.

Calculation: chocolate grams × total methylxanthines (mg/g) ÷ patient kg. Semisweet/sweet dark retains the 5.3–5.6 mg/g range; other listed types use Merck’s approximate values. Cocoa-% estimate = 15.5 mg/g × cocoa fraction, an approximation rather than a brand assay. Brands and recipes vary.

Mass units: 1 avoirdupois oz = 28.349523125 g. Both input units use the same mg/g model, so converting units preserves exposure. Merck’s separately rounded mg/oz table may therefore differ slightly. All entered products are added; a partly completed entry prevents a complete estimate. This models ingested amount without subtracting emesis or estimating absorbed dose.

NESTLÉ Toll House semi-sweet morsels label: 1 Tbsp = 14 g. Derived rough portions: 2 Tbsp (⅛ US cup) ≈ 28 g ≈ 1 oz; ¼ US cup ≈ 56 g ≈ 2 oz. Chip size and packing vary. Bar-square weights vary by product; use package mass divided by equal pieces. This source supports portion estimates only; methylxanthine values and risk references remain Merck-based.
